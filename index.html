Key Finding

The oil–equity correlation is not stable. It swings from +0.43 during the demand-driven oil price war to −0.92 during geopolitical supply shocks. A single static correlation figure is fundamentally insufficient to characterize this relationship.

Key Finding — Supply vs Demand

During the Iran War (supply shock), oil beta was strongly predictive: Energy stocks outperformed, Airlines underperformed. During the COVID crash (demand shock), oil beta was not predictive — all sectors fell together. The direction of causality, not just magnitude, determines the cross-sectional pattern.

Section 3 — Methodology

Python Package Architecture

Built from scratch using only pandas, numpy, and matplotlib. No external statistical packages.

data_loader.py

Reads Bloomberg Excel data, cleans dates, merges CL1 and SPX into a single DataFrame. Also handles yfinance downloads for Part B.

analyzer.py

Computes ln(P(t)/P(t−1)) log returns, rolling 30-day Pearson correlation, OLS via NumPy normal equations, and shock detection at |r| > 3%.

events.py

Defines eight event windows. Computes per-event cumulative returns, within-event correlation, and summary statistics for Part A.

report.py

Generates eight publication-ready matplotlib charts and a formatted console report. All figures in the PDF were produced here.

multi_stock.py

Downloads 26 stocks via yfinance. Runs Stock = α + β₁·Oil + β₂·SPX + β₃·VIX + ε for each. Produces sector heatmaps and beta rankings.
